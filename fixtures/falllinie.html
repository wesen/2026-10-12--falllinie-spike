Fall­linie

German for the fall line, the straightest way down. The mountain here is a function with no last value, the soundtrack is written while you ride, and the run lasts as long as you do. Fill the meter with tricks, then go über.

Design notes

01Research

What SSX does

  • Tricks fill an adrenaline meter that doubles as boost, so tricking is also how you go fast.
  • SSX Tricky (2001) added über tricks, absurd moves where the board often leaves the feet, unlocked when the meter is full.
  • Landing six übers spelled T-R-I-C-K-Y and gave unlimited boost for the rest of the race.
  • SSX 3 (2003) doubled points for varied combos, cut points for repeated tricks, and chained airs, rails and presses into one combo line.

What this prototype keeps

  • Meter = boost = über gate. Full meter + Shift + grab starts an über.
  • Each landed über lights one letter of G-R-O-T-E-S-K. All seven invert the world and give 30 s of unlimited boost.
  • Combos multiply by trick count, repeats decay by 0.6ⁿ, bails drop the combo.
  • No finish line: the slope is infinite, so score and distance are the goals.

Sources: SSX Tricky · SSX 3 · GameSpot on SSX 3 scoring · SSX wiki

02Game design

Loop

  • Ride down an endless course that bends left and right, with banks, halfpipe sections, kickers, mega ramps, cliff drops, rails and trees.
  • Air → spin, flip, grab → land clean → combo grows → meter fills → boost or über.
  • Land with the board under you and pointing along your path, regular or switch. Otherwise you bail and lose the combo.
  • A combo banks 2.2 s after the last clean landing. Rails keep it alive.

03World math

Height field

y(x,z) = B(z) + wall(|u| − W/2) + roll + feat + mogul
u = x − c(z)                      lateral offset
s(z) = 0.30 + 0.07 sin(z/190) + 0.04 sin(z/77+1.3)
B(z) = −∫s = −(0.30z − 13.3 cos(z/190) − 3.08 cos(z/77+1.3))
c(z) = 55 sin(z/310) + 22 sin(z/127+2.1) + 8 sin(z/53+.4)

The slope and the course centre are sums of sines, so both integrate in closed form and any metre of the mountain is computed on demand, without state.

Walls and features

wall(d) = R − √(R² − d²)          d < 0.92R
        = 0.608R + 2.35(d − 0.92R) past it, soft-capped
kicker  = H((a+L)/L)²             a ∈ [−L, 0], lip at a = 0
cliff   = D·smooth(−Lp, −0.3Lp, a) then drop over 1.6 m
cell    = 110 m, seeded: mulberry32(hash(c, seed))

Halfpipe cells use R ≈ 5.5 and narrow W; cruiser cells use R 11–19. Parameters cross-fade over the last quarter of each cell.

Riding

ground: v ← v + (g − (g·n)n)Δt,  v ⊥ n
edge grip: v_side ← v_side·e^(−7Δt), speed kept 90%
drag: 0.55 + 0.0042|v|² m/s²  (tuck 0.0026)
leave ground when v·n > 1.2 + 0.12|v| or gap > 0.35 m
air: g = 13 m/s², spin 7.2 rad/s, flip 6 rad/s
legs and arms: two-bone IK, law of cosines

04Art direction

Swiss print on snow

  • Two inks on paper. The key ink draws rider, trees, rails, outlines and painted markings. The spot ink prints halftone, contour lines and the sun.
  • Shading becomes a 45° halftone screen; light never becomes a gradient.
  • Contour lines at 0.55 m of relative height draw the terrain like a topographic map.
  • Outlines come from depth creases and material changes, like a technical drawing.
  • Distance is painted on the snow every 250 m in condensed four-digit numerals.
  • GROTESK mode swaps paper and key ink: dark snow, light lines.

Palettes

Every scheme is neutral or cool paper, one key ink and one vivid spot ink, as in two-colour screen printing. C cycles them. X-ray draws the maths over the scene: survey grid, course lines, feature equations, skeleton and IK, vectors, predicted flight and live equations.

Type

Archivo at 62% width for numerals and trick names, 125% width for banners. IBM Plex Mono for data labels.

05Sound

Written live in WebAudio

  • 86 BPM, 58% MPC-style swing, snares 12 ms late, humanised hats.
  • FM electric piano on minor ninth chords, sine sub bass, vinyl crackle and tape wow.
  • Formant vocal chops through a dotted-eighth delay, pumped by the kick.
  • Glitch sections with ratchets, bit-crushed rolls and blips. GROTESK adds an acid line.

Tied to the ride

  • In the air the drums drop out and a low-pass closes. Landing brings the beat back with a sub hit.
  • A clean trick answers with a vocal arpeggio in the current chord.
  • A bail sweeps the filter shut and scratches.
  • Carve, wind and rail grind are filtered noise driven by speed.
